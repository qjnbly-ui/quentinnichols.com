Knowledge is abundant and, for me, one of the most enjoyable ways to shape our understanding of the world and our character. The books listed below have had a profound impact on how I think and perceive life - some to a great extent, while others sparked a deeper curiosity. I want my friends to understand who I am, not just through my personal experiences (which you can read about elsewhere on the site), but also through the ideas and lessons I have gathered from the experiences of others.

I have come to realize that almost everything we go through in life has been lived by someone before us. Books offer a shortcut to gaining those insights. By sharing this collection, I hope to provide a window into how these works have influenced me and invite you to explore them too.

From The Ruthless Elimination of Hurry

Biggest Takeaway

Hurry isn’t just moving fast. It’s a state of mind that keeps us from fully experiencing the present moment and from noticing God, other people, and even our own lives.

Reflection

What made this book different was John Mark Comer himself. He doesn’t write as someone who has life completely figured out. Instead, he openly shares his own struggles with hurry, distraction, ambition, and the pressures of modern life. That honesty made his message feel genuine and relatable.

One of the ideas that stayed with me most was how Jesus was never in a hurry. Even with endless demands on His time, He was fully present with the people in front of Him. He regularly withdrew to pray, made time with the Father even during His busiest days, walked instead of rushed, and never seemed controlled by urgency. It challenged me to realize that being busy isn’t the same as living well.

The book also gave me a new appreciation for the Sabbath. The idea of intentionally setting aside time to rest, worship, and simply be present with God feels incredibly meaningful in a culture that celebrates constant productivity. I hope to make that a reality in my own life someday.

This book made me question how often I’m physically present but mentally somewhere else, thinking about what’s next instead of appreciating where I am. Slowing down isn’t about accomplishing less; it’s about becoming more present with God, with others, and with the life I’ve already been given.

From Dopamine Nation

Biggest Takeaway

Pleasure and pain are part of the same system. The more I chase constant stimulation, the harder it becomes to enjoy ordinary life.

Reflection

This book completely changed how I viewed my own habits. I always thought my struggles were about nicotine or my phone individually, but I came to realize they were all expressions of the same reward system. It explained why I could quit one habit only to immediately crave another.

Instead of asking, “How do I avoid discomfort?” I’ve started asking, “What kind of discomfort is actually helping me grow?” Working out, staying busy, and resisting constant stimulation have become ways of retraining my brain rather than simply exercising discipline. The goal isn’t to eliminate discomfort; it’s to choose the kinds of discomfort that make life richer and more rewarding over time.

Favorite Quotes
