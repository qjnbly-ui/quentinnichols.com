Everything Feels Possible

I'm on my way to work this morning. I'm running late, but the roads are nice, it's a beautiful winter morning, and I just feel incredibly thankful.

I've been so overwhelmed lately, but at the same time, I don't know if I've ever been this excited about what I'm doing or where my life is going.

The last couple of weeks have been incredibly busy. For a long time, I was going to bed around 9:30 or 10. Then it became 10:30. Now I'm regularly staying up until 11:30, midnight, or even later, and waking up around 5:30.

I'm exhausted sometimes. It's getting harder to get out of bed because I'm not sleeping enough. But what's interesting is that I'm also more excited to wake up than I've ever been. I wake up thinking about everything I could accomplish that day.

There just aren't enough hours.

And I think that's what has brought me to this next stage of building N3XRA.

Building something bigger than myself

Right now, I'm working on what I've been calling a conference room of AI agents.

I want a dashboard where I can essentially have an entire team of AI employees. Different agents responsible for different things: managing current projects, organizing future ideas, researching, analyzing, tracking spending, monitoring budgets, and coordinating development.

I want to incorporate OpenAI, Anthropic's Claude, xAI's Grok, and whatever other systems make sense. I don't want to be dependent on one model or one company. I want the system to intelligently determine which model is best for a particular job, while also considering cost.

Most of the actual coding could be delegated to Codex, while other agents coordinate, research, review, and manage the work.

I want these agents to understand what I'm building, what needs to be done, what the priorities are, and how much money is available. I want them to help me make decisions, not just wait for instructions.

The ultimate goal is to build a system that allows me to accomplish far more without personally managing every little detail.

And eventually, I want this intelligence to exist throughout N3XRA.

We're getting closer to releasing AI directly into each organization's dashboard, where customers will be able to purchase credits and use these capabilities themselves.

But my vision goes so much further than that.

One intelligence, everywhere

Something I've been thinking about is that I don't want Nex to become a collection of separate AI assistants.

I want one foundational intelligence that works throughout the entire N3XRA ecosystem.

At the platform level, Nex understands N3XRA, its applications, and how to operate them.

At the organization level, it understands how that particular organization works, its policies, its processes, and its preferences.

At the individual level, it recognizes the person it's helping, understands their permissions, and adapts to how they prefer to interact.

And eventually, I want to take that same intelligence into the physical world.

Imagine an organization being able to download a local Nex application, install it on a computer, connect its devices, cameras, microphones, lighting, heating systems, and other equipment, and have an intelligent assistant that understands and operates the entire environment.

Someone could walk into the recreation center and say, "Hey Nex, check me in."

Nex could recognize their enrolled voice, verify their identity when necessary, check their membership, and sign them in.

That same Nex could help me manage the recreation center, control its equipment, review finances, organize projects, or work on software.

It wouldn't be a separate assistant for every application or device.

It would be the same intelligence, operating with different capabilities depending on where it is, who it's helping, and what it's authorized to do.

And I don't think the local and cloud systems need to feel separate. Some processing could happen on a local computer, while more demanding tasks use cloud models. They could share the same organizational context and permissions while keeping sensitive information protected.

Of course, people should have control over what Nex remembers, what devices it can access, and whether features like voice recognition are enabled.

But the foundation would remain the same.

One Nex. One shared foundation. Different contexts, permissions, and capabilities.

I think this is one of the most important architectural decisions I can make right now.

Because if I build Nex correctly, every application I create in the future could inherit its capabilities.

Every improvement to Nex could benefit the entire ecosystem.

I wouldn't have to keep rebuilding the same intelligence.

And eventually, N3XRA wouldn't just be a place where people manage their websites, records, utilities, businesses, and organizations.

It could become a platform that intelligently helps them operate all of those things, including the physical environments around them.

That's what I'm really trying to build.

Bringing that intelligence into my everyday life

One of my biggest goals is to eventually replace Alexa at the recreation center with something I've built myself.

I want an assistant that understands the building, controls the lights and heat, recognizes people and their preferences, manages music, and connects to all the systems I've created.

I want to be able to walk into the gym, talk to Nex naturally, and have it understand what I'm asking without having to build a specific command or automation for every possible interaction.

And I want that same technology in my home.

I like Alexa, but there's something so much more exciting about having a system I've built myself. Something I can customize, improve, connect to new devices, and develop over time.

I want to own the system, control the data, develop the intelligence, and keep improving it.

For years, I've wanted to build things like my own security cameras, smart devices, and automation systems. Those ideas always seemed like projects for some distant future when I had more knowledge, more resources, or an entire team.

Now I look at them and think: I could actually build that.

Not necessarily tomorrow. Not without a tremendous amount of learning and work.

But it's obtainable.

And that's an extraordinary feeling.

N3XRA is my foundation

One of the biggest things I've had to learn is that I need to stop building everything separately.

Every time I have an idea, I start thinking about creating another application. But why?

N3XRA already provides the infrastructure.

If I spend a little more time building something properly within N3XRA, suddenly that capability can become available to everyone.

That's the whole point.

The recreation center application has well over 100,000 lines of code, probably closer to 150,000. And every time I improve N3XRA's AI, I find myself having to go back and improve the recreation center's AI separately.

I hate that duplication.

Every time I learn something or develop a better approach, I don't want to have to implement it in five different places.

I want to build things once, build them well, and allow everything else to benefit.

That's why N3XRA is so important to me.

I've thought about what it would mean to eventually sell the company, and I honestly don't know that I could.

N3XRA isn't just a business I'm building to sell. It's the infrastructure for my creativity. It's where I can build almost anything I imagine, now and for the rest of my life.

If I sold it and wanted to start creating again, I'd have to rebuild so much of that foundation. I could easily spend another six to ten months just getting back to where I was.

It's becoming something deeply personal.

It's the platform that allows me to pursue whatever creative ideas I have in the future.

And I don't think I ever want to lose that.

Half a million lines of learning

N3XRA has 500,691 lines of code across 3,345 files.

Sometimes I think about how much of that could be cleaned up. How much smaller or more efficient it might be if everything were organized perfectly.

I know it's messy.

I know there are things I would have done differently if I'd understood what I know now.

I would have made different architectural decisions. I would have chosen some different technologies and programming languages. I would have structured things better from the beginning.

But I didn't know those things then.

I learned by building.

And once you've established hundreds of thousands of lines of code, changing the foundation becomes a much bigger undertaking.

I've been trying to improve things as I go, but I also have to recognize how far I've come.

Every mistake taught me something.

Every problem forced me to understand something I didn't understand before.

And all of that experience is what allows me to imagine building things today that I wouldn't have known how to approach even a year ago.

From wanting to learn to code to this

When I was 18, I wanted to learn how to code.

I've always been fascinated by technology. I've wanted to build my own devices, cameras, software, automation systems, and all kinds of things.

For so long, those ideas seemed like things that required enormous teams, specialized education, or millions of dollars.

Now I look at them and think: I could actually build that.

I want more computers. I want to experiment with hardware, chips, local AI models, and custom programming. I want to understand these systems at a deeper level.

And the amazing thing is that I don't really see an end to what I could create.

Every new thing I learn opens up ten more possibilities.

Every tool I build gives me the ability to build something else.

And the technology itself is evolving so quickly that the possibilities keep expanding.

There are companies with enormous teams and enormous amounts of money working on these same technologies.

But that doesn't discourage me.

I think we're entering a period where individuals will be able to accomplish things that previously required entire companies.

People will have access to intelligence that can teach them, help them develop ideas, and give them the ability to build their own tools.

I think traditional education is going to have to change dramatically because of this.

For someone who wants to learn how to build software, there are now opportunities to learn by doing that simply didn't exist before.

You can have an idea, ask questions, experiment, fail, learn, and keep going.

And I feel incredibly fortunate to be learning these things while they're evolving so quickly.

Why I want to understand it myself

Eventually, I could have a team of people helping me build all of this.

And I think that would be incredible.

But right now, I think there's something extremely valuable about doing as much as I can myself.

I want to encounter the problems.

I want to understand why things fail.

I want to learn how the systems fit together.

I want to understand the infrastructure, the architecture, the programming, the hardware, the business, and the decisions that go into building all of it.

I don't want to build a company where everyone else understands the technology better than I do.

Not because I need to be the smartest person in the room, but because I genuinely love learning this stuff.

I want to know how it works.

I want to be able to make informed decisions and understand the consequences of those decisions.

And if I eventually have a team, I want to be able to work alongside them with a real understanding of what we're building.

That's part of why this conference room of AI agents is so exciting to me.

It's not just about getting more work done.

It's about learning how to organize intelligence, delegate responsibilities, coordinate complex projects, and build systems that can operate with increasing independence.

I think learning how to do that now could become one of the most valuable things I ever do.

The challenge of having endless possibilities

I think the biggest things holding me back right now are time, organization, and eventually funding.

There are so many things I want to build that I sometimes struggle to decide what deserves my attention first.

And that's frustrating because I can see how all these pieces could eventually fit together.

I can imagine what N3XRA could become.

I can imagine Nex operating throughout the platform, managing projects, helping customers, controlling buildings, and assisting with development.

I can imagine having more computers, more resources, and eventually more people helping bring these ideas to life.

But I still have to get up and go to work.

I still have responsibilities outside of N3XRA.

And there are only so many hours in a day.

That's why I want to build the systems that help me manage all of this.

I don't want to spend my time constantly switching between projects, remembering what needs to be done, checking whether something deployed, or repeating the same work across different applications.

I want to create a structure that allows me to focus on the things that actually require my attention.

And I know that getting there is going to take time.

I also know I need to find a better balance.

I can't keep sacrificing sleep indefinitely just because I'm excited about what I'm building.

There's something almost funny about being so excited to wake up that I don't want to go to bed.

But I want to be able to enjoy this journey.

I don't want to exhaust myself trying to accomplish everything at once.

And I think part of becoming better at all of this is learning that I don't have to.

An incredible time to be alive

Sometimes I think about how different my life could have been.

How many little decisions, experiences, mistakes, and opportunities brought me to this exact moment.

From being a kid who wanted to learn how to code to now building a platform with hundreds of thousands of lines of code, developing AI systems, working with real organizations, and imagining technologies I once thought were completely out of reach.

It's wild.

And the most exciting part is that I feel like I'm just getting started.

I don't know exactly what N3XRA will look like in five years.

I don't know how capable Nex will become.

I don't know what technologies will exist that we haven't even imagined yet.

But for the first time, I genuinely feel like almost anything I want to create is within reach if I'm willing to put in the time, learn what I need to learn, and keep going.

That's an incredible feeling.

And this morning, driving to work, looking at how beautiful everything is, I just feel grateful.

Grateful for the experiences that brought me here.

Grateful for everything I've learned, including the mistakes.

Grateful for the people and opportunities that have helped me along the way.

Grateful that I get to live at a time when so much of this is becoming possible.

I'm overwhelmed, absolutely.

I'm tired.

I have more ideas than I know what to do with, and sometimes I wish there were twice as many hours in a day.

But I'm also incredibly happy to be doing what I'm doing.

I don't know exactly where all of this is going to take me.

And for once, that's one of the most exciting parts.

I just want to keep learning, keep creating, and keep building.
