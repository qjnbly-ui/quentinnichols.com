Seeing Michelangelo and the Sistine Chapel Again

Anytime I read about these things, it just makes them feel closer. Something that took place in the Renaissance feels no further apart than yesterday feels to me. They are both in the past. I can read the documentation of what took place, or recall something from memory, and to me it is no different. Reading about it just puts it into perspective. Wow, this is incredible.

I was watching this documentary about Michelangelo, and it is fascinating to think about these being real men living in that era. I have spent so much of my time learning about early Christianity, and then the Renaissance feels like this refining of Greek and Roman culture within a Christian world that is still evolving. Just such an interesting time. And then Michelangelo and da Vinci competing with one another. I never knew that.

Then to think about Michelangelo having such a relationship with Pope Julius II. The Sistine Chapel ceiling was finished in 1512, and the Pope died just a few months later, in 1513. Then Michelangelo went back to working on his tomb. It is fascinating how he goes from this historic artist to this guy who actually lived. These were people he knew. This was his life.

And the ceiling itself. I always thought that was the architecture, and there were paintings inside of it. I thought they were all individual paintings, just grids of paintings. Then to realize he painted that architecture too, and painted the scenes into the architecture he created. Even things that look like statues are painted. It is crazy to think what it actually is.

I would have thought he would set scaffolding up across the whole chapel, paint all the fake architecture, keeping it geometric, and then paint the scenery into it. But that is not how he worked. He worked from one end toward the other, in sections. And it is , so it is pigment going into fresh plaster. It is not just paint on a finished ceiling. It is this plaster. That is wild.

And then there is the account of him painting smaller figures initially, looking at them from the ground, and realizing you could not really make them out. As he got to the other end, the figures became larger. Just think about him down there, looking up at what he had been working on, and then going back up and changing how he did it.

To me, that change in scale has its own effect on the space. You have larger figures at one end and smaller figures at the other, and looking through the room in one direction can make it feel bigger, while looking the other way can make it feel smaller. Or vice versa, however that works out from where you are standing. That is the part I find so interesting: the size of the figures becomes part of how you read the room.

Somehow I had seen this ceiling so many times and never actually taken the time to see it. The architecture, the paintings inside it, the figures that seem to sit in front of it. And all of this coming from a guy who actually lived, working his way across fresh plaster. It is just fascinating.

Fresco

A painting technique in which pigments mixed with water are applied to fresh lime plaster. As the plaster sets, the pigment becomes part of the surface. The artist works in prepared sections called giornate, rather than plastering the entire ceiling at once.
