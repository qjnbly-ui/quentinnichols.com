A Reminder of How Quickly Life Can Change

As first responders, we never know when the next call might involve someone we love.

This morning, just before 7:00 AM, Bly Rural Fire Protection District was dispatched to a motor vehicle accident involving a vehicle that had gone off a steep embankment, with the driver reportedly ejected.

As I was preparing to respond, I received a phone call from my uncle. He said, "Quentin, just so you know, this is your brother."

My brother, Luke, had been involved in a serious accident around 1:00 AM while traveling back to camp. His vehicle left a rough, washboarded gravel road and nosedived down a steep canyon at an angle. Not wearing a seatbelt, Luke was ejected through the windshield as the vehicle continued to roll down the hillside.

During the ejection, he likely struck the steering wheel and windshield, sustaining injuries later determined to include a fractured hip, multiple broken ribs, and a broken collarbone.

Despite these injuries and wearing only a T-shirt, Luke managed to crawl down to where his vehicle had come to rest to retrieve additional clothing and keep himself warm. He then attempted to crawl back up toward the road but was unable to make any progress due to the pain. He spent the remainder of the night exposed to the cold, shining his flashlight and calling out as vehicles passed above. Several hours later, someone finally noticed him and stopped to help.

When we arrived, our focus was getting him safely out of that canyon and into the care he needed. It was a difficult rescue, requiring nearly two hours of teamwork, equipment, and people willing to step in wherever they were needed.

I want to personally thank Chiloquin Fire & Rescue, Klamath County Fire District No. 1's rope rescue team, Klamath Tribal Police, my fellow members of Bly Rural Fire Protection District, and the many community members and passing motorists who stopped to lend a hand. Every person who contributed played a part in getting my brother to safety.

As a firefighter, I've responded to many calls, but today was a reminder that the person we're responding to could just as easily be one of our own family members. In those moments, all we can do is remain focused, rely on our training, work together, and do everything we can for the person who needs us.

Looking at the distance Luke traveled down that hillside, the condition of his vehicle, and everything he endured through the night, I am incredibly thankful that he is still here with us today.

This is also a reminder to all of us to be careful on the roads, particularly on rough, washboarded gravel roads where conditions can change quickly. It only takes a moment for life to change in ways we could never anticipate.

And perhaps most importantly, have compassion for the people around you. Take care of one another, lend a hand when you can, and never take the people in your life for granted.

Thank you again to everyone who helped my brother today. Our family is incredibly grateful.

Quentin Nichols
Bly Rural Fire Protection District
